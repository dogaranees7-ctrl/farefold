# Food containers and trays — design research

> Internal design research for FareFold. These format directions are not claims about FareFold's current manufacturing methods, material approvals, certifications, or available stock. Do not reuse third-party photography, logos, artwork, or proprietary dielines without permission.

## Design brief

Develop a flexible takeaway range for rice meals, curries, salads, snacks, desserts, and mixed portions. Design the container and lid as a matched system. The correct format depends on food temperature, moisture, oil content, portion size, delivery time, reheating needs, and the intended service environment.

## Container and tray directions

### 1. Hinged clamshell container
A one-piece base and attached lid with a snap or tuck closure. Useful as a general takeaway format where fast packing and a simple open/close experience matter. Explore shallow and deep versions and validate the hinge, rim, and closure after repeated handling.

### 2. Single-compartment meal tray
An open tray or lidded container for a main meal. Focus on usable volume, corner geometry, stiffness, and a lid that remains secure during delivery. Avoid oversizing the package relative to the portion.

### 3. Two- to five-compartment meal tray
Integrated dividers keep rice, curry, sides, and condiments apart. Compartment layouts should be derived from actual menu portions, not generic equal-sized wells. Test divider height, liquid transfer, and how food is loaded and eaten.

### 4. Bowl with fitted lid
Round or square bowl formats can suit salads, rice, noodles, desserts, and other meals. Explore rolled or reinforced rims and compatible flat or dome lids. Verify that the lid remains secure when the bowl is carried and stacked.

### 5. Soup / liquid-food container
A deeper vessel with a secure lid and sufficient wall stiffness. Validate leak resistance with the actual hot or cold contents, normal fill level, transport orientation, and expected delivery duration. A vented lid should not be treated as leakproof.

### 6. Salad / cold-food container
A clear or opaque base with a fitted lid can present cold meals and layered foods. Consider headspace, condensation, dressing separation, and whether a separate sauce cup is preferable.

### 7. Snack and side trays
Smaller formats for fries, nuggets, pastries, fruit, and sides. A shallow open tray can speed service; a lidded version may be more appropriate for delivery. Test whether vents preserve texture without allowing excessive cooling or spillage.

### 8. Serving-convertible tray
A tray with a removable or fold-back cover can transition from transport to serving. Treat this as a concept for customer-specific development: verify structural stability, opening sequence, food access, and safe handling before adopting it.

### 9. Protective molded-fibre insert
A shaped insert can hold multiple items in place within a larger carton. Explore this for fragile or separated foods only where the tooling, material, and food-contact suitability are established.

## Lid and closure directions

- Snap-fit lid: convenient for matched rigid containers; test rim tolerances, retention, and opening force.
- Hinged lid: reduces loose components and can speed packing; test hinge fatigue and closure reliability.
- Overlap / press-on paperboard lid: can offer a printable surface; test fit and barrier performance against moisture and oil.
- Separate compartment insert: can keep sauces or toppings apart; ensure the insert cannot shift during transport.
- Controlled steam release: vent placement should be tailored to the food and journey. More ventilation is not automatically better.
- Tamper-evident feature: consider only where a defined customer requirement and suitable validated closure design exist.

## Material and use-case selection

Do not treat paperboard, molded fibre, PP, PET, CPET, aluminium, or barrier-coated constructions as interchangeable. Each has different forming, barrier, temperature, sealing, and end-of-life characteristics. Select the construction only after defining the actual food and use conditions.

Microwaveable, ovenable, freezer-safe, leakproof, greaseproof, compostable, recyclable, and food-safe are performance or compliance claims. Do not use them in FareFold customer-facing copy unless the exact finished construction has supporting documentation and appropriate test evidence. In particular, never infer microwave suitability from a generic paper or plastic appearance.

## Prototyping and validation checklist

- Record food type, portion mass, dimensions, fill temperature, moisture / oil content, and expected holding and delivery time.
- Define whether the product is for hot fill, cold fill, reheating, freezing, or ambient service before selecting material.
- Confirm food-contact suitability for every component: base, lid, coating, inks, adhesives, seals, and inserts.
- Test leakage and migration of liquids at realistic fill levels, orientations, handling, and delivery durations.
- Evaluate condensation, steam management, grease penetration, odour / taint, and food texture after realistic hold times.
- Measure usable capacity and headspace; check that the lid does not touch the food.
- Test closure retention, opening force, stack height, nesting, compression, drop / tilt handling, and delivery-bag fit.
- For compartment trays, test divider integrity and liquid transfer between wells.
- For microwave or oven claims, test the complete finished package under defined conditions and include safe-use instructions where required.
- Check print rub resistance, colour consistency, scuffing, and artwork distortion on formed surfaces.
- Review recycling or composting claims against the full construction and actual local collection and processing availability.
- Make physical prototypes, document results, and obtain customer approval before production.
