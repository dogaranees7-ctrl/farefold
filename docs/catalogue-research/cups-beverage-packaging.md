# Cups and beverage packaging — design research

> Internal research for FareFold. Reference examples are not claims about FareFold's current production capabilities, material approvals, certifications, or product range. Do not reuse third-party photography, artwork, logos, or proprietary structures without permission.

## Design brief

Create a coherent beverage packaging family that covers hot drinks, cold drinks, and dessert beverages. The cup, lid, sleeve, carrier, and straw opening should be treated as a compatible system rather than isolated products. Fit and performance must be verified with physical samples.

## Cup construction directions

### 1. Single-wall hot cup
A straightforward printed cup for applications where the selected board and barrier are suitable for the filling temperature and hand-contact conditions. Test whether a separate sleeve is needed for comfort.

### 2. Double-wall cup
An outer paper layer adds a second wall and an insulating air space. Explore a clean smooth-wall appearance with branding across the full wrap. Validate seam integrity, rim roundness, nesting, and lid fit.

### 3. Ripple-wall cup
A textured outer layer offers grip and hand insulation and can reduce the need for a separate sleeve. Test the ripple profile, outer-wall attachment, stack/nesting behaviour, and consistent appearance across sizes.

### 4. Cold-drink cup
Specify the intended beverage temperature, condensation exposure, fill volume, and lid style. Validate barrier performance, sidewall stiffness, bottom seam integrity, and print resistance under condensation.

### 5. Dessert / ice-cream cup
A wider opening and shorter body may suit scoops, toppings, and thick drinks. Match the lid and rim carefully; check spoon access and stacking for takeaway.

## Lid and accessory directions

### 1. Sip-through hot lid
A fitted lid with a drinking aperture. Check snap retention, drinking comfort, leakage during normal carrying, and compatibility with the exact cup rim.

### 2. Straw-slot cold lid
A lid with a straw opening or cross-slit. Test the intended straw diameter and retention, splash behaviour, and compatibility with the selected cold cup.

### 3. Flat / dome lid
Compare a low-profile lid for standard cold beverages with a dome lid where toppings or foam require additional headspace. Do not assume the two lids share a rim fit.

### 4. Paperboard lid exploration
Paper-based pressed or folded lids are an active design direction in the market. Evaluate their actual barrier construction and fit; some products described as paper lids still include a film layer.

### 5. Cup sleeve
A separate sleeve can add grip, insulation, and a large branding area. Design it for a defined cup taper and test fit across the intended cup sizes.

### 6. Cup carrier
A multi-cup carrier can reduce spills and make orders easier to transport. Prototype handle strength, cup-hole fit, centre of gravity, and the number of cups supported.

## Coordinated visual systems

- Build one visual identity across cup, lid, sleeve, carrier, and takeaway bag.
- Explore kraft / uncoated looks, minimal typography, bold colour blocking, and seasonal artwork as distinct creative routes.
- Keep legal and product information legible and avoid environmental claims unless the complete material construction and local end-of-life route support them.
- Use original FareFold artwork and customer-approved brand assets.

## Prototyping and validation checklist

- Confirm capacity and dimensions: top diameter, bottom diameter, height, rim profile, and usable fill volume.
- Define the beverage: hot or cold, actual fill temperature, acidity / fat content where relevant, hold duration, and expected handling.
- Obtain documented food-contact suitability for the cup stock, barrier, inks, adhesives, lid, and any sleeve or carrier in the intended use.
- Test liquid leakage at side seam and bottom seam, rim roundness, lid retention, and carrying / tilt behaviour.
- For cold drinks, test condensation and moisture exposure; for hot drinks, test hand comfort and outer-wall temperature under realistic service conditions.
- Check cup nesting, de-nesting, dispenser compatibility, sleeve fit, lid fit, and transport stability.
- Confirm print rub resistance, colour consistency, and artwork distortion around the cup taper.
- Review recyclability / compostability only against the full construction and the collection / processing systems actually available to the intended customer.
- Approve physical samples before production and retain the tested sample and specification record.
