# Bags and food wraps — design research

> Internal design research for FareFold. These directions are not claims about FareFold's current materials, production methods, certifications, or available stock. Do not reuse third-party photography, logos, artwork, or proprietary structures without permission.

## Design brief

Build a family of bags, wraps, liners, and carry formats for takeaway, bakery, cafe, and food retail use. Distinguish direct-food-contact bags from outer carry bags. A bag that carries a closed food box does not automatically need the same barrier properties as a wrapper touching hot, greasy food.

## Bag directions

### 1. Flat paper bag
A simple compact format for dry bakery items, snacks, and pre-wrapped food. Explore flat-bottom and side-gusset variations based on product thickness and loading speed.

### 2. SOS / self-opening satchel bag
A square-bottom bag that can stand open for faster service. Useful for bakery, takeaway, and counter service. Validate base construction, opening behaviour, seam strength, and load capacity.

### 3. Grease-resistant food bag
A direct-contact bag for sandwiches, pastries, fried snacks, or similar foods. Choose the grease barrier based on actual food, temperature, contact time, and pressure. Paper weight alone does not establish grease resistance.

### 4. Window bakery / deli bag
A bag with a transparent window to show the product. Explore centred or offset windows, optional micro-perforation, and an anti-fog approach where suitable. Validate window-film food-contact status, sealing, visibility, and moisture behaviour.

### 5. Vented hot-food bag
A format for selected hot products where controlled moisture release may help preserve texture. Test vent size and position against actual products and delivery time; vents can also allow heat loss or leakage.

### 6. Paper carry bag
An outer carrier for cups, cartons, and takeaway orders. Explore flat handles, twisted-paper handles, handleless gusseted bags, and reinforced bases. The handle attachment and base fold must be tested with realistic loads.

### 7. Bottle / cup carrier bag
A gusseted bag or insert with dividers to limit movement. Test fit, centre of gravity, handle comfort, and whether the carrier accommodates different cup or bottle sizes.

### 8. Bakery and bread bag
A breathable format for selected crusty breads, or a higher-barrier construction for soft bread where moisture retention is important. The best design depends on the baked product and intended shelf / holding time.

## Wrap and liner directions

### 1. Grease-resistant sandwich / burger wrap
A sheet or pre-cut wrap that can fold around a product quickly. Explore printed patterns, portion marks, and fold guides while ensuring the ink system is suitable for the intended contact use.

### 2. Bakery tissue / interleaving sheet
A lightweight sheet for lining trays or separating pastries, confectionery, and baked items. Confirm the exact grade is intended for food contact; ordinary tissue is not automatically suitable.

### 3. Greaseproof liner sheet
A liner for baskets, trays, and boxes. Specify grease performance for the actual food and contact conditions, and test whether the liner transfers odour, ink, or coating.

### 4. Pouch / envelope wrap
A folded sleeve or envelope for sandwiches, wraps, cookies, and slices. A partially open top can improve access and visibility; a full closure may be better for transport. Match the format to the food's moisture and temperature.

### 5. Printed brand tissue
A decorative inner wrap can add a premium unboxing layer. Use only suitable food-contact paper and inks if it touches food; otherwise keep it clearly separated from direct food contact.

## Construction and specification notes

- Flat, satchel, SOS, gusseted, windowed, handled, and wrap formats solve different packing and carrying needs.
- Paper basis weight (GSM) is only one variable. Fibre furnish, sizing, grease treatment, coatings, seams, base folds, handle patches, humidity, and storage all affect performance.
- Window film, coatings, inks, and adhesives must be assessed as part of the complete package.
- Breathability and barrier performance are trade-offs: a construction that helps a crusty product release moisture may not be right for soft bread or a sauced item.
- Environmental claims must describe the complete construction and the local collection / recycling or composting route. Do not infer recyclability or compostability from a paper appearance.

## Prototyping and validation checklist

- Define whether the bag is direct food contact or secondary carry packaging.
- Record food type, dimensions, mass, temperature, oil / moisture content, and contact duration.
- Test grease staining and migration with actual menu items; evaluate at realistic time and temperature.
- Test wet strength, seam strength, base integrity, handle tear-out, and carry performance with realistic loads.
- For windows, test adhesion, seal integrity, fogging, clarity, and compatibility with food-contact requirements.
- For vented bags, compare crispness, temperature, moisture, and spill behaviour against a non-vented control.
- Test the actual folding / packing sequence and confirm the format works at service speed.
- Evaluate print rub, ink transfer, odour, and artwork legibility on the selected substrate.
- Confirm documentation for paper, barriers, films, inks, and adhesives under the intended conditions of use.
- Make samples, run customer-specific hold and delivery trials, document results, and obtain approval before production.
