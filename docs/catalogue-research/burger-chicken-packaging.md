# Burger and fried-chicken packaging — design research

> Internal design research for FareFold. These are reference concepts, not claims about FareFold's current materials, certifications, production methods, or validated performance. Do not reuse third-party product photography, logos, dielines, or distinctive protected artwork without permission.

## Design brief

Develop a coordinated takeaway range for burger and fried-chicken businesses. Prioritise fast packing, secure closures, fit to the food portion, grease management, controlled moisture release, stackability, and a recognisable branded surface. The best structure depends on the menu, holding time, delivery journey, and selected food-contact material.

## Burger box directions

### 1. Classic hinged clamshell
A one-piece foldable box with an attached lid and locking tabs. It is a familiar, quick-to-pack starting point for single burgers, cheeseburgers, and sliders. Explore low-profile and taller variants so the lid does not compress taller builds.

### 2. Vented delivery clamshell
Retain the hinged lid but test small, strategically placed steam-release openings. Vent position and area should be evaluated against the actual burger, hold time, and delivery conditions: excessive ventilation can cool the food, while a fully sealed box can trap moisture.

### 3. Tall / gourmet burger box
A higher sidewall and lid clearance for stacked burgers. Check that the closure does not touch toppings and that the base resists deformation when stacked or carried.

### 4. Burger-and-sides meal box
A divided or modular structure for a burger with fries or a side portion. Validate divider height, food movement, packing speed, and whether separate compartments are practical for the target order.

### 5. Branded burger wrap
A grease-resistant wrap sheet or sleeve can complement the box, simplify handling, and provide a second branding surface. The barrier specification must match the food and contact conditions.

## Fried-chicken directions

### 1. Snack / individual portion carton
A compact tuck-top or hinged carton for wings, tenders, or a small bone-in portion. Prioritise a stable base, secure closure, and controlled vents.

### 2. Family / sharing carton
A larger, reinforced carton with a broad opening for mixed chicken portions. Prototype carrying and stacking loads with realistic food weights; avoid assuming a larger box can simply use the same board and folds as a snack carton.

### 3. Ventilated bucket
A tapered bucket format with a fitted lid is a strong visual direction for sharing portions. A published Detpak / KFC Go Bucket case describes side-wall gills and lid openings intended to create an airflow path for steam release. Treat this as a functional reference, not a design to copy.

### 4. Handle / gable carrier
A handled carton for combo meals or sharing orders. Test handle tear-out, closure security, and whether the handle interferes with venting or stacking.

### 5. Fries, tenders, and sauce accessories
A small open or partially covered side carton, plus a separate sauce cup or pocket where the menu needs it. Keep sauces isolated from crisp products during delivery.

## Prototyping and validation checklist

- Capture the real food dimensions, mass, height, oil level, and normal packing method before drawing a dieline.
- Test at least two board grades / constructions selected by a packaging technologist for the intended application; do not copy a competitor's GSM or coating specification as a universal recipe.
- Confirm food-contact suitability for the actual board, barrier, inks, adhesives, and intended contact conditions with supplier documentation and applicable local requirements.
- Evaluate grease penetration, leakage, odour / taint, condensation, and surface staining using the actual menu items.
- Compare vent patterns and opening sizes through controlled hot-hold and delivery simulations; assess crispness, temperature, and moisture rather than assuming more vents are always better.
- Test closure retention, one-hand opening, packing speed, flat storage, erection, stacking, compression, and motorcycle-delivery handling.
- Check print scuffing, rub resistance, colour consistency, and artwork legibility on the selected stock.
- Review recyclability or compostability claims against the complete construction, including coatings, windows, adhesives, and local collection infrastructure.
- Make physical prototypes and obtain customer approval before production. Record the test conditions and results.
